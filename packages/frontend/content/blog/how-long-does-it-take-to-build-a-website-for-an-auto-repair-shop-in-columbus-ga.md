Expect roughly 2 to 5 weeks for an auto repair shop website, according to [a scoping guide for auto repair sites](https://dev.to/amanhstu/scoping-an-auto-repair-shop-website-where-the-budget-really-goes-42nf). [Elementor](https://elementor.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-build-a-website/) puts a standard multi-page small business site at about 4 to 8 weeks. The biggest swing factor is how quickly you hand over your text, photos and logins.

## How long does a website for an auto repair shop take?

Two sources give two ranges, and both are reasonable. The auto repair guide above gives 2 to 5 weeks for a shop site. Elementor's 4 to 8 weeks is for a standard multi-page small business site built by a professional.

The gap comes from scope. A shop with a handful of services, one location and a phone number for booking sits at the short end. A shop that wants online scheduling, an estimate tool or a connection to its shop software sits at the long end.

Treat these as ballparks, not promises. Your own timeline depends on the project and on how fast decisions get made, which is mostly in your hands.

## How long does a one-page website take to build?

None of the sources here gives a separate figure for a one-page site, so there is no honest number to quote. The logic is simple, though. The auto repair guide lists the number of service pages as something that adds time. One page means less to write, less to photograph and less to review.

A one-page site can work for a small shop that needs to show its services, hours, location and phone number. If you want a single page, ask any designer for a timeline in writing before you start.

## What slows down a small business website build?

The most common cause is waiting on the owner. [Elementor](https://elementor.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-build-a-website/) names missing client text and images as the biggest cause of project delays. [Wilkes & Wood](https://wilkeswood.com/blog/how-long-does-a-website-take) agrees that content is usually the variable that moves the timeline.

For an auto repair shop, [the scoping guide](https://dev.to/amanhstu/scoping-an-auto-repair-shop-website-where-the-budget-really-goes-42nf) lists what adds time:

- More service pages
- Online scheduling
- Estimate tools
- Connections to shop software
- More than one location

Logins are the other common holdup. Wilkes & Wood notes that domain, DNS, hosting, email and account access can hold up a launch if nobody knows where the logins are. DNS is the setting that points your web address at your website.

## Do I need to own my domain name before the build starts?

Your domain name is your web address, such as yourshopname.com. According to the [Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-your-business-name), you register it through a registrar, you have to renew it regularly, and owning it protects your business's web address.

The SBA source doesn't say you must own it before a build begins. Still, it helps to sort it out early, because the domain is one of the things that can stall a launch. Before the project starts, find out:

1. Whether you already own a domain, and who registered it. Sometimes a nephew, a former employee or an old web company did it.
2. Which email address the registrar account uses.
3. Whether you can log in, or at least reset the password.

The SBA's piece on [hiring a developer versus using a DIY builder](https://www.sba.gov/blog/which-better-hiring-web-developer-or-using-diy-website-builder) also covers using your own domain name. Whichever route you take, make sure the domain is registered in your name.

## What do I need to give a web designer before they start?

Gather these first, since missing content is the usual cause of delay:

- **Your services, in plain words.** Oil changes, brakes, tires, diagnostics, or whatever you actually do.
- **Business basics.** Name, address, phone number, hours and the areas you serve.
- **Photos.** The shop, the team and the front of the building. Real photos usually beat stock images.
- **Logins.** Domain registrar, current website host if you have one, and the email account tied to them.
- **Decisions on extras.** Whether you want online scheduling or an estimate request form, and what software the shop already uses.

If your shop works from a home address, the earlier post [Should You Hide Your Home Address on Google Business Profile?](/blog/should-i-hide-my-home-address-on-my-google-business-profile) is worth reading before you decide what to publish.

## What pages should an auto repair shop website have?

The sources don't prescribe a page list, so treat this as common sense. Most customers want to know what you fix, where you are, when you're open and how to reach you. That points to:

- A home page
- A services page, or a few pages if you offer distinct services
- A contact page with your address, hours, phone number and a map

The more pages you add, the more writing and review time you add. Start with what customers ask about on the phone. A package that covers up to 5 pages, such as {{name:business}}, is plenty for many shops, and {{name:starter}} covers a single page. You can see every option on the [pricing page](/pricing).

## Is a template builder faster than hiring a professional?

Usually, yes. [Network Solutions](https://www.networksolutions.com/blog/automotive-website/) compares a basic template-builder auto repair site, at 8 to 15 hours spread over a few days, with 4 to 8 weeks for professional design.

Speed isn't the only difference. The SBA's article on [hiring a developer or using a DIY builder](https://www.sba.gov/blog/which-better-hiring-web-developer-or-using-diy-website-builder) covers tradeoffs such as ongoing support, hosting and upkeep. If you're in Columbus and want to see what a local build looks like, the [web design in Columbus, GA](/web-design/columbus-ga) page explains how it works.

## What should you do next?

Spend twenty minutes on a short checklist. Write down your services, your hours, your phone number and address, and where your domain is registered. Then try to log in to that account. If you can, your site is unlikely to stall on the things that most often cause delays.
