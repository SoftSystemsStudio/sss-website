A Facebook page can be enough if your routes are full and every customer finds you through people you already know. It stops being enough when you want new customers to find you, or when you can't afford to lose your only listing. A simple one-page website covers those gaps.

## Do I need a website if I get work from Facebook?

Not necessarily, and lawn care owners don't agree on it. In [a long LawnSite discussion](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/lets-get-this-straight-yes-or-no-to-needing-a-web-site-for-lawn-business.445761/page-3), some owners say Facebook is plenty for steady routes. Others say a site matters once you want to grow or look credible to people who have never heard of you.

So the real question is what you want from the next year:

- **Keep the same customers, same routes:** a well-kept Facebook page and good word of mouth may do the job.
- **Add new neighbourhoods or bigger jobs:** you will want something you own that a stranger can check before they call.

## What happens if my Facebook page is restricted?

A Facebook page runs on rules set by someone else. Meta says that [Pages which repeatedly break its policies can have their distribution reduced or lose features](https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/restricting-accounts/). That doesn't mean it will happen to you. It means you have no say in how the page is treated if it does.

If your page is the only place your customers, photos, reviews and contact details live, a restriction leaves you with very little to fall back on. A website you control gives you a second place to send people.

## What does Google use to decide who shows up for "lawn care near me"?

Google says local results are [based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). It also says that complete, detailed business information helps it match a business to what people search for.

In plain terms: the more clearly you say what you do and where you do it, the easier it is for Google to match you with the right searcher. A page that lists your services, the towns you cover and how to reach you gives Google more to work with than a feed of recent posts.

Nobody can promise a particular ranking, and this post won't either. The point is that clear, complete information helps.

## How does a website connect to my Google listing?

Your Google Business Profile, the listing customers see on Search and Maps, [has a field for your website](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en). When someone finds you there, they can tap through to a page that explains your services and shows how to get a quote.

If you don't have a website, that part of the listing has nothing of your own to point to. If you are weighing up what to put on that listing, our post on [whether to hide your home address on Google Business Profile](/blog/should-i-hide-my-home-address-on-my-google-business-profile) covers another decision that comes up at the same time.

## What is the minimum website a lawn care business needs?

One page is enough to start. It should answer the questions a homeowner has before calling:

1. What do you do? (mowing, edging, leaf cleanup, landscaping beds, and so on)
2. Which towns do you serve?
3. How do I reach you? A phone number that can be tapped on a phone, and a simple request form if you like.
4. What does it look like? A handful of your own before-and-after photos.
5. How do I get a price? Whether you quote by phone, by form, or list rates. Our post on [whether a lawn care website should list prices or ask for a quote](/blog/should-a-lawn-care-website-list-prices-or-just-ask-for-a-quote) goes through that choice.

## Who owns my web address?

You do, if you register it in your own name. The Small Business Administration explains that [a domain name is registered through a registrar, can differ from your legal business name, and has to be renewed regularly](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-your-business-name). Two practical points follow from that:

- Make sure the registration is under your name and email, not only your web designer's.
- Put the renewal date in your calendar so the address doesn't lapse.

A short, easy address is also simple to put on a truck, a yard sign or an invoice.

## Should I link my Facebook page and my website together?

Yes, there is no reason to choose one. Keep posting jobs and photos on Facebook, where your current customers already are. Put a link to your Facebook page on your website, and put your website address in your page's contact details. Each then helps the other, and neither is your only copy of your business information.

## Quick answer: which one fits you?

- **Facebook alone** can work if you are full, rarely get calls from strangers, and are comfortable depending on a platform you don't control.
- **Facebook plus a one-page site** fits if you want new customers, want to look established, or want a backup that is entirely yours.

## Your next step

Open Google on your phone and search for the kind of lawn care you offer plus your town, such as Opelika or Phenix City. Look at what comes up, and at what a homeowner would see if they clicked your listing. Then write down the five items from the list above and note which ones your Facebook page answers clearly today. The gaps are what a one-page site would need to fill.
