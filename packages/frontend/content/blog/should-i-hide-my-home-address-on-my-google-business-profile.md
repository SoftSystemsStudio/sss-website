If you run a business from your house, the address on your Google Business Profile can feel like an invitation for strangers to knock on your door. The short answer: if customers never come to your home, hide it and list the towns you serve instead. If they do come to you, the answer is a little different. This post explains how to tell which situation you are in.

## Your address on Google is public

Whatever address you put on your profile can be seen by anyone who looks you up. Google's page on [managing your business address](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2853879?hl=en) says this directly, and it adds that only service-area businesses should hide their address.

A service-area business is one that goes to its customers rather than the other way around. Think plumbers, lawn crews, mobile detailers, house cleaners, electricians and mobile pet groomers. Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) use a plumber who works from home as their example of a business that should keep its address hidden from customers.

## Three situations, three answers

### 1. You travel to customers and nobody visits your home

Hide the address and list the areas you serve. Google's help page on [service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) says a business that does not serve customers at its address should remove the address and list only the service area.

This is the setup for most home-based trades. Your home stays private, and your profile still tells people where you work.

### 2. Customers come to your location and you also travel

This is sometimes called a hybrid business. A shop with a storefront that also delivers or makes house calls is one example. Google's service area page says businesses that do serve customers at their address should enter both the address and the service area.

This only makes sense if you have a place customers can actually visit. A home where you occasionally meet a client is a decision for you to think through carefully, and it is worth reading Google's guidelines before you decide.

### 3. Customers come to you and you never travel

Then you are a regular storefront-style business, and your address should be visible so people can find you. Hiding it would work against you.

## How to list your service area

If you are in the first or second group, you will need to tell Google where you work. A few things to know:

- **Areas are set by city or postal code, not by radius.** Google's [service area page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) says so, so you will not be drawing a circle of so many miles around your house.
- **Be realistic.** Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) limit how far a service area can extend. Listing every town in the region you might drive to on a good day is not the goal.
- **Do not leave it blank.** If you do not enter a service area, [Google picks one for you](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2853879?hl=en). It may not match where you actually want to work.

Say you run a handyman business out of Smiths Station. You might list Smiths Station, Phenix City, Opelika and Auburn if those are the towns you regularly drive to, and leave out places you would turn down because of the distance. Choose the list based on where you really want jobs, not on how many names you can fit.

## Hiding the address: what to expect

Google's [address management page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2853879?hl=en) gives the steps for turning off the display of your address, so follow those rather than a screenshot from an old video, since menus change.

Before that, you will likely need to confirm your business with Google. The [verification page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en) lists the ways this can happen: phone, email, video or postcard. Which ones you are offered varies by business, so you may not see all four. If a postcard is your option, it goes to the address you entered, so make sure you can receive mail there and plan for some waiting time.

## A short checklist

1. Ask yourself whether customers ever come to your address for service.
2. If not, remove the address from public view and enter your service area.
3. If yes, enter both your address and your service area.
4. List towns by name or postal code, and keep the list to places you really serve.
5. Complete verification using whichever option Google offers you.
6. Check your profile afterward the way a customer would, to confirm your home address is not showing and your towns are.

## Keep the rest of your information consistent

Hiding your address on Google does not do anything to the rest of the internet. If your home address is printed on your website, social media pages or an old directory listing, people can still find it there. It is worth a quick look at those places and removing it where you can, and listing the same towns you chose on Google so your information matches. This is common-sense housekeeping, not a Google requirement.

## The bottom line

If you work from home and go to your customers, hiding your address and listing your service area is what Google expects, and it keeps your front door out of public view. If customers visit you at a real location, show the address, and add the service area if you also travel. Either way, take a few minutes to choose your towns carefully, since that list is what tells nearby customers you cover their part of the map.
