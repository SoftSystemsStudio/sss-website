You do not always have to pay a monthly fee to keep a salon website up after it is built, but you do have to keep paying for a domain (your web address) and for hosting (the computer space that serves your site to visitors). Updates and a monthly care plan are usually optional extras.

## Do I have to pay monthly for a salon website?

Not necessarily for the website itself. What you owe depends on how the site was set up.

Some costs are required, because without them the site stops working. Others are optional, because they pay for someone's time rather than for keeping the site online. The trouble for most salon owners is that a quote can mix the two together in one line, so it is hard to tell which is which.

The sections below separate them so you can ask for each one by name.

## What are the real ongoing costs of a salon website?

According to [GoDaddy's guide to website maintenance costs](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/website-maintenance-cost), ongoing costs come down to hosting, domain renewal and maintenance. [Network Solutions](https://www.networksolutions.com/blog/website-maintenance-cost/) lays out a similar breakdown of domain, hosting, updates and optional maintenance services, so you can see which ones you must pay and which you can skip.

In plain terms:

- **Domain renewal (required).** This is what keeps your web address yours.
- **Hosting (required).** This keeps the site visible online. GoDaddy notes that hosting costs vary widely between shared plans and managed plans, so the price you pay depends on which kind you are on.
- **Updates and edits (optional).** New prices, a new stylist, holiday hours, a fresh photo. You can do these yourself if your site makes that easy, or pay someone.
- **A maintenance or care plan (optional).** A monthly fee for someone to handle changes and look after the site for you.

Only the first two are needed to keep a site online. The last two are about convenience.

## What happens if I stop paying for my domain?

This is the one cost you should never let slip. A domain has to be renewed before it expires for your website and any email address built on it to keep working. If it is not renewed, [ICANN explains](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/domain-name-renewal-expiration-faqs-2018-12-07-en) that it may be released so that anyone can register it.

For a salon, that could mean your booking link stops working and emails from clients bounce. [Network Solutions describes the same outcome](https://www.networksolutions.com/help/article/what-happens-if-i-dont-renew-my-domain): the site goes down, email stops, and getting the domain back after the grace period costs extra.

You do get some warning. [ICANN's summary of its expired registration policy](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/registrant-about-errp-2018-12-07-en) says registrars must send renewal reminders before expiry, and there are grace and restore periods. Those reminders go to the contact email on the domain record, so that email needs to be one you actually read. A old address or a former employee's inbox defeats the purpose.

## Who should own my salon website's domain and hosting account?

You should. The domain and hosting accounts should be in your name, with your email address as the contact, even if someone else sets them up and manages them.

This is common-sense protection. If you own the accounts, you can move to a different designer later, and renewal reminders reach you directly. If they sit in a designer's account, you depend on that person to keep paying and keep answering.

Before you sign anything, ask in writing:

1. Whose name is the domain registered in?
2. Whose email receives the renewal reminders?
3. Who holds the hosting account, and can you get the login?
4. What happens to the site if you and the designer part ways?

A written agreement is the right place for those answers. Our earlier post on [whether you need a written contract with a web designer](/blog/do-you-need-a-written-contract-for-a-one-page-business-website) covers what to look for.

## Should I be paying a separate hosting bill at all?

It depends on how the site is built. On a hosted website builder, hosting is bundled in. [Squarespace's pricing page](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing) shows secure hosting and SSL (the security certificate behind the padlock in a browser) as part of the monthly subscription.

So if your site lives on a builder like that, a second hosting charge from a designer is a fair thing to ask about. Ask what exactly it covers and why you are paying for it twice.

If your site is not on a builder, a hosting charge is normal. The point is simply to know what each line on your bill is for.

## Is a care plan worth it for a one-page salon site?

It can be, but it is a choice, not a requirement. A one-page site with your services, hours, location and a booking link changes rarely. If you are comfortable updating it yourself, or you only need a change a few times a year, paying monthly may not make sense.

A plan is more useful if you change prices or your stylist list often, you do not want to log in and edit anything, or you would rather have someone watching the site than find out about a problem from a client.

## What should I ask before I pay for anything monthly?

When you get a quote, or review the one you already have, check these points:

- Which charges are required to keep the site online, and which are optional?
- What does each recurring charge actually pay for?
- Are the domain and hosting in your name?
- When do renewals happen, and who is reminded?
- Can you cancel the optional parts and keep the site?

## Your next step

Write down every website charge you pay or have been quoted. Next to each one, mark it as required (domain, hosting) or optional (edits, care plan). Then log in to your domain account and confirm that the contact email is one you check. That ten-minute check protects the one cost you cannot afford to miss.
