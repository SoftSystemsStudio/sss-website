## Why name towns across the river at all?

Your customers do not think about the state line the way a map does. The City of Phenix City says that Phenix City and Russell County are [the only Alabama county in the Columbus Metropolitan Area](https://phenixcityal.gov/doing-business/), which is a good reason to treat both sides of the river as one customer area.

A homeowner in Smiths Station or Phenix City may search with their own town's name, and they want to know quickly whether you come out to them. If your site only says "Columbus", some of them will wonder and move on. Naming the town answers the question before they have to ask it.

The same goes for Auburn and Opelika if your work reaches that far. Say so plainly.

## How many towns should I list on my website?

List the places you genuinely serve, and stop there. Google Business Profile allows [up to 20 service areas, set by city or postal code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en), and its guidance is to be specific and accurate. That is a sensible rule for your website too.

A short, honest list beats a long one. If you would turn down a job in a town, leave it off.

A simple way to present it:

- One sentence near the top of the home page, such as "Serving Columbus, Phenix City and Smiths Station."
- A short "Where we work" section on your contact or services page, with the towns written out.
- Your phone number and hours close by, so the list leads somewhere.

## Should my website match my Google Business Profile service areas?

Yes, as far as you can. If your profile says one thing and your website says another, customers and Google both get a muddled picture of where you work. Treat the two as one list and update them together.

How you describe your area also depends on the kind of business you run. Google separates [service-area businesses, which travel to customers, from hybrid businesses, which also serve people at their own address](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514743?hl=en). Work out which one you are before you write your list.

If you work from home and are unsure what to show publicly, our earlier post on [whether to hide your home address on Google Business Profile](/blog/should-i-hide-my-home-address-on-my-google-business-profile) covers that side of it.

## Do I need a separate website page for every town I serve?

No. For most small businesses, one clear service-area list does the job. Separate pages only make sense when a town gives you something real to say.

There is also a risk in overdoing it. Google's spam policies describe doorway abuse as [pages made to rank for similar searches that send visitors to a less useful page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). A stack of near-identical pages that differ only in the town name fits that description, and it does nothing for the person reading.

## What makes a town page useful instead of spammy?

Ask one question: would a customer in that town be glad they landed on this page? If so, it is probably useful. A town page earns its place when it has details that cannot be copied to the next town by changing one word.

Good signs:

- It covers the services you actually offer in that town, and any that you do not.
- It gives practical details such as how you schedule visits there, or where customers can meet you.
- It has its own content, not the same paragraphs as every other town page.
- It links back to your main services and contact details, so the visitor can act.

Warning signs:

- Ten pages with the same text and a different town name.
- Pages for towns you do not serve.
- Lists of place names stuffed into a paragraph.

If you cannot write more than a sentence or two that is true only for that town, put the town in your service-area list instead.

## Should I list my service area if customers come to my shop?

It depends on whether you also go to them. If people only visit your shop, your address, directions and a note about which side of the river you are on matter more than a service-area list. Mentioning that you are easy to reach from Phenix City or Smiths Station is fine, as long as it is true.

If you do both, you fit the hybrid description above. Show your location and list the areas you travel to.

## A quick check you can do today

Open your website and your Google Business Profile side by side. Write down every town each one names, then compare the two lists. Cross off any town you would not really serve, add any you do, and make both match. Then check that the towns appear in readable sentences on your site, not just in a footer.
